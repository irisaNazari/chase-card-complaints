Dana must choose which 1–2 credit card customer pain points to fund next quarter, so that Chase's card complaints to the CFPB stop showing up as an issue in risk reviews.

## Situation: 

Chase is one of the largest U.S. credit card issuers, and its card customers can file complaints with the CFPB, which publishes them in a public database. Dana leads Card Customer Experience and has budget next quarter to fix customer pain points.

## Complication:

 Card complaints to the CFPB keep coming up in leadership's risk reviews, but there's no clear view of which pain points drive them, whether they're getting better or worse, or how Chase compares to peers. Without that, the budget could go to the wrong fix.

## Question: Which 1–2 card pain points should Dana fund next quarter to make the biggest dent in CFPB complaints?

Trend window: 2018 through 2025 (full years), with 2026 year-to-date shown separately. I am starting from 2018 because in April 2017 the CFPB changed its product and issue categories(credit cards were grouped with prepaid cards). 2018 is the first full year under the newer system. 2019 normal year, but 2020 and 2021 Covid time so spending dropped. Stopping at 2025 since 2026 is not over. About 8 years is enough. It shows a real direction without going so far back that the card business looked different.

## Questions:

1 - Which CFPB issue categories drive the most Chase credit card complaints (by count and share), and which carry the most severity (by rate of monetary relief), from 2018 to 2025?
2- Are complaints in those top categories rising or falling, year by year from 2018 to 2025, with 2026 compared year-to-date?
3- How does Chase's complaint rate per card account compare to the 5–8 largest U.S. card issuers, overall and in the top categories?

## Hypotheses

H1 (top issue): I think purchase disputes are Chase's top card complaint issue, because customers must gather proof and wait on the bank's decision, which is slow and frustrating. I'd be wrong if disputes aren't in the top 2 issues by count from 2018 to 2025.

H2 (trend): I think Chase's card complaint count got worse from 2018 to 2025, because leadership keeps seeing it in risk reviews and complaints to the CFPB overall have grown as more people learned they can file. But I think the rate per account grew less, since Chase also added customers. I'd be wrong if the count was flat or falling, or if the rate rose faster than the count.

H3 (peers): I think Chase's complaint rate per account is higher than its big peers, because a large, well-known brand with many digital users attracts more CFPB filings per customer. I'd be wrong if Chase's rate is below the peer median.

## Scope and limits

**In scope**
- Chase credit card complaints in the CFPB database, 2018–2025 (2026 year-to-date shown separately)
- Top complaint issues by count, share, and severity (monetary relief rate)
- Trend over time, as count and as rate per account
- Comparison with the 5–8 largest U.S. card issuers, per account

**Out of scope**
- How to fix each pain point and what it costs (needs internal data; next step for card operations after Dana chooses)
- Other Chase products (mortgages, checking, auto loans)
- Complaints Chase receives directly, which aren't in the CFPB data

**Limits of the data**
- Complaints are not a random sample: only customers who know about the CFPB and choose to file are counted
- The CFPB does not verify that complaints are true
- Complaint text is only published when the customer agrees, so text analysis covers a subset
- Company responses (like "closed with monetary relief") are reported by the company itself
- Category names changed over time, so labels must be checked before comparing years
